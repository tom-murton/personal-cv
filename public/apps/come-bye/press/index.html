Description

Come Bye is a sheepdog game about a year on a Lakeland hill farm. You herd with one finger, the way a shepherd works: draw the line and your border collie runs it, wide round the flock, and the sheep move away from the dog. “Come bye” is the shepherd's call that sends a dog round a flock, and in the game it is the first thing you do. Then the year goes on: lambing in spring, summer, autumn and winter work, a Show each season against rivals with faces, repairs you watch go up, and an evening in the farm kitchen when the day is done.

  • The drawn line. Real herding craft in a one-finger game. Sheep move away from the dog, so the skill is placing the dog, drawing the line and giving the flock room, not tapping fast.
  • A year that remembers. Lambs born and named, an adopted second dog, the farm's repairs and the Shows you won or lost carry through the year, from the local show to the Supreme.
  • Fair by design. A job that runs out of time costs pay, never sheep. Sheep are at risk only when a job's card warns of a hazard first.
